# Overclaim-Control Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist records the final manuscript claim discipline for the AI-MLA
Studio prototype manuscript. Its purpose is to keep the paper credible,
reviewer-readable, and aligned with the evidence generated by the synthetic
benchmark.

## Manuscript Identity

Use this identity consistently:

> A synthetic mechanism benchmark for testing when hierarchical operational
> state improves AI misuse monitoring, escalation routing, and analyst workload
> relative to flat scoring and simpler aggregate baselines.

## Supported Claims

The manuscript can claim:

- The benchmark tests whether preserving hierarchical operational state helps
  when harmful signals are weak locally but coherent across accounts,
  signatures, campaigns, or system-level patterns.
- In the current synthetic benchmark, learned hierarchical state improves
  held-out account-review harm capture relative to learned flat baselines at
  the main review-budget operating point.
- Targeted ablations show that uncertainty, signature/campaign state, and
  provenance each suppress a distinct matched failure mode.
- Learned hierarchical logistic models are better calibrated than the current
  learned flat logistic baseline in the held-out synthetic scenarios.
- System-alert state improves true-exploit ranking in the current benchmark,
  but does not yet provide reliable top-k product-alert surfacing.
- Validation-tuned hierarchical routing improves escalation quality across
  `auto_handle`, `human_review`, `policy_review`, and
  `product_investigation` relative to learned flat routing.
- Learned hierarchical routing suppresses adversarial false-systemic product
  escalation better than brittle rule hierarchy in the designed hard-negative
  families.
- Workload, deduplication, backlog, and priority-queue simulations show that
  routing quality changes analyst burden and time-to-alert under the benchmark
  assumptions.
- A larger held-out replication run preserves the main directional claims while
  exposing scale-sensitive weaknesses.

## Claims Not Made

The manuscript should not claim:

- Hierarchy is universally better than flat scoring.
- AI-MLA Studio is a new computational paradigm.
- The current prototype is deployment-ready or production-ready.
- The benchmark proves real-world monitoring performance.
- The current system-alert layer reliably catches product-level exploits in
  top-k alert slots.
- The routing policy is mature enough for operational use.
- The workload simulation is a staffing estimate.
- The synthetic scenarios are representative of all real misuse or benign
  coordination patterns.
- The current learned baselines are the strongest possible ML baselines.

## Preferred Wording

Use:

- "synthetic mechanism benchmark"
- "tests whether"
- "provides conditional evidence"
- "under controlled scenarios"
- "held-out synthetic scenarios"
- "directional stability"
- "bounded contribution"
- "requires external validation"
- "relative to flat scoring and simpler aggregate baselines"

Avoid:

- "new paradigm"
- "proves"
- "deployment-ready"
- "production-ready"
- "general solution"
- "solves AI safety"
- "real-world performance" unless explicitly qualified as not established
- "universally better"

## Reviewer-Risk Notes

Likely reviewer concerns and current responses:

- **Synthetic-only benchmark:** state clearly that the study isolates
  mechanisms and cannot establish deployment performance.
- **Baseline strength:** acknowledge that dependency-free learned models are
  prototype baselines and that stronger calibrated ML baselines are needed.
- **System-alert fragility:** present exploit-rank improvement separately from
  top-k alert surfacing.
- **Routing policy simplicity:** describe validation tuning, but keep the caveat
  that the route policy class remains threshold-based.
- **Workload realism:** frame analyst workload and queue simulations as
  practical probes, not staffing estimates.
- **False systemic alerts:** keep adversarial benign coordination visible as a
  central failure mode rather than a secondary limitation.

## External Validation Requirement

Before any deployment-facing claim, the manuscript should be extended with at
least one of:

- additional independent seeds and larger scenario families
- stronger calibrated learned baselines
- controlled red-team or simulation traces
- public moderation, abuse-detection, or incident-response datasets if
  licensing and safety constraints permit
- an interactive review dashboard evaluated with realistic analyst workflows
